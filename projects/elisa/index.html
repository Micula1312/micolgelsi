Elisa

An audiovisual performance exploring hypervisibility, oversharing and the anxiety generated by fragmented digital identities. Through live interactions between the performer's body, digital devices and an Instagram image archive, the work investigates fluidity as a possible response to digital exposure, questioning the boundaries between bodies, images and data across physical and virtual ecosystems.

ELISA is a live audiovisual performance investigating hypervisibility, oversharing and the anxiety generated by fragmented digital identities within contemporary social media ecosystems.

Taking an Instagram image archive as its starting point, the work explores how bodies, identities and personal experiences are continuously translated into images and data, circulating through systems of exposure, accumulation and algorithmic mediation.

Through live interactions between the performer’s body, digital devices and visual materials, the performance develops an experimental investigation into the relationships between physical gestures, technological interfaces and image transformation. Scrolling, fragmentation, displacement and generative processes progressively destabilize the screen, allowing images to detach from their two-dimensional surfaces and migrate into fluid, three-dimensional environments.

Rather than treating technology as a neutral instrument, ELISA approaches the interface as an active component of the performative process, where bodies, devices and images mutually influence and transform one another.

Within this shifting environment, fluidity emerges as a possible response to the pressures of digital exposure and fragmentation, opening questions about permeability, interdependence and the continuous reconfiguration of bodies and data across physical and virtual ecosystems.
