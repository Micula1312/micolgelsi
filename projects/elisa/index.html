MICOL GELSI

Multimedia artist and researcher working across public and digital spaces.
Her practice brings found and reclaimed materials into dialogue with low-tech tools and digital technologies.
Through living archives, site-specific interventions and participatory methodologies, she explores collective memory, inclusion and access to digital culture.
Eco-systems, territories, liminality and power recur as fields of investigation.
She founded ASAP and BLIVET and is a member of Sound Studies Forum.

Elisa

The performance stems from a reflection on contemporary forms of control and the possibility of envisioning new forms of female emancipation. By exploring themes such as the relationship between the body, identity, and the construction of imagery, the work examines the cultural and technological mechanisms that shape our perception of reality. The figure of Aicha Kandisha serves as a bridge between myth and the present, prompting a reflection on the narratives that have historically shaped the female body. The reference to “hypnocracy” introduces a critique of the mechanisms through which images, language, and media steer desires and behaviors, suggesting a need to reclaim spaces for awareness and self-determination.
